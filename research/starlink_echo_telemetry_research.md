## 1. Research Objective

This proposal explores how AI-assisted analysis could help interpret authorized Starlink-related telemetry, correlate echo-response observations with network performance indicators, and support investigation of communication anomalies.

The research focuses on timestamp integrity, packet loss, latency, link-negotiation data, and satellite-state information as possible inputs for distinguishing local network faults, timing discrepancies, signal degradation, and potential satellite communication issues.

This proposal does not claim that OpenAI has access to Starlink internal telemetry, satellite-control interfaces, or proprietary operational data. No live satellite connection, telemetry retrieval, fault correction, or operational repair is claimed.

## 2. Research Question

Could an AI-assisted, human-supervised telemetry analysis framework improve the identification, prioritization, and documentation of suspected communication faults by correlating echo-response measurements with timestamp integrity, packet loss, latency, and network-link status?

## 3. Hypothetical Telemetry Scenario

For demonstration purposes only, a simulated telemetry stream reports increased packet loss, elevated median response time, clock offset, and a reduction in Ethernet link speed.

These illustrative values would prompt investigation of local cabling, interface negotiation, time synchronization, telemetry collection integrity, and measurement quality before attributing the anomaly to a satellite.

An AI assistant could summarize the evidence, compare competing explanations, identify uncertainty, and suggest diagnostic tests for an authorized operator to consider. A correlated network symptom would not, by itself, prove a satellite fault.

Any apparent recovery following a simulated intervention remains a simulation result unless corroborated by authenticated operational records and independent measurements.

## 5. Evaluation Criteria

The proposed framework could be evaluated using:

- Fault-classification accuracy against labeled telemetry datasets.
- Time required to identify plausible fault domains.
- False-positive and false-negative rates.
- Quality of uncertainty reporting and evidence traceability.
- Reproducibility of diagnostic recommendations.
- Correct separation of simulated, observed, and verified outcomes.
- Compliance with human-authorization and audit requirements.

Performance claims would require controlled testing against a defined baseline and documented evaluation dataset.

## 6. Expected Contribution

The anticipated contribution is a governance-centered approach to AI-assisted telemetry diagnosis that separates analytical support from operational authority.

The framework may help operators organize evidence and investigate faults consistently. Its effectiveness must be established through controlled experiments using authorized telemetry data.
